Glossary

Benefits jargon decoder

Plain-English meanings for the words on your SBC and paycheck deduction. Educational only — not advice.

Premium

What you (and often your employer) pay to keep coverage in force. On the calculator, enter your share only.

Deductible

What you pay for covered care before most plan benefits kick in. Preventive care is often covered before you meet it.

Out-of-pocket maximum

The most you’d typically pay in a year for covered in-network care. After you hit it, the plan usually pays 100% of covered in-network services for the rest of the year.

Copay

A flat dollar amount you pay for a specific service — like a doctor visit or generic Rx — often after (or sometimes instead of) the deductible, depending on the plan.

Coinsurance

Your percentage of the bill after the deductible — for example, you pay 20% and the plan pays 80% — until you reach the out-of-pocket maximum.

In-network

Providers and facilities that have contracted rates with your plan. Using them usually means lower costs and counting toward deductible and OOP max.

Out-of-network

Care outside the plan’s contracted network. Costs are often higher, may not count the same way toward your OOP max, and can include balance billing.

Allowed amount

The amount your plan recognizes for a service. In-network, it’s usually the contracted rate. Bills above that may leave you with extra cost out-of-network.

Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC)

A standard, short plan summary. Use it to confirm deductible, OOP max, and examples of coverage — then verify those numbers in tools like the liability calculator.

Formulary

The plan’s list of covered prescription drugs, usually in tiers (generic, preferred brand, specialty) with different cost shares.

Prior authorization

When the plan must approve a service or drug before it will pay. Skipping it can mean denied claims.

Explanation of Benefits (EOB)

Not a bill. A statement of what was billed, what the plan allowed, what it paid, and what you may owe.

HSA

Health Savings Account. Tax-advantaged dollars you own, usually paired with a high-deductible plan. Can help offset deductible and OOP costs.

FSA

Flexible Spending Account. Employer-sponsored account for eligible medical expenses. Use-it-or-lose-it rules often apply; different from an HSA.

Open enrollment

The yearly window when you can usually change medical, dental, vision, and related elections without a qualifying life event.

Qualifying life event

A change — like marriage, birth, or loss of coverage — that can open a special enrollment period outside open enrollment.

Definitions are general. Your plan documents control. For a rough worst-case cash picture, try the healthcare liability calculator.
